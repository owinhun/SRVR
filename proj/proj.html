What Does Waveform Audio Add Once a Speech-Time Reference Is Given?

A Factorial Study of Cross-Modal Temporal Binding

Seunghun Oh*  ·  Jaehong Myung*  ·  Unsang Park†

* Equal contribution  ·  † Corresponding author
Sogang University · Department of Artificial Intelligence / Department of Computer Science and Engineering

The research question

When speech tells us when to look, does hearing it still help?

Narration can describe a future or past action rather than the action visible at the moment of speech. Waveform audio tells a model both when speech happens and what was spoken—two roles that conventional audio-removal comparisons cannot separate.

We provide an external speech-time reference and independently vary whether the waveform is available at answer time. This matched factorial identifies the measured additional effect of waveform access under a fixed reference. It does not establish that removing audio is a general remedy.

Method

The same question, four matched inputs

A 2×2 comparison crosses speech-time reference availability with waveform audio access on 291 held-out CMTB items. An additional 48 items are used only for development.

Primary results

Waveform effects are model-dependent

On the main CMTB evaluation, the most robust reference-fixed reduction is observed for Qwen2.5-Omni. Other estimates are smaller, inconclusive, correction-sensitive, or sensitive to parsing failures.

Reference information recovers visual answers

With waveform retained, reference additions increase accuracy point estimates by +6.2 to +26.8 pp under numeric references and +26.5 to +34.4 pp under localized references.

Qwen2.5 shows the clearest additional cost

With the reference fixed, waveform access lowers Qwen2.5 accuracy by 15.1 pp; a negative direction also appears in its bare-timestamp and localized controls.

What the controls support

Shifting reference timestamps away from speech time sharply lowers video-based accuracy. Error analyses indicate more selections of narrated alternatives, without isolating a single waveform mechanism.

What external tests do not show

On selected-speech CrossTask (N=93) and COIN (N=85) items, paired tests are inconclusive. No general advantage from removing audio is established.

Interpretation

A diagnostic comparison, not an audio-off recipe

The factorial is designed to separate the gain from supplying a speech-time reference from the incremental effect of waveform access under that same reference. These are different questions, and a negative interaction is not evidence of an intrinsic audio-harm mechanism.

Important limits. CMTB is a targeted stress test; numeric references include answering instructions; model input pipelines are not identical; some estimates depend on correction choices or failed outputs; and external selected-speech tasks do not reproduce a general removal advantage. The manuscript reports the exact statistical families, controls, and sensitivity analyses.
